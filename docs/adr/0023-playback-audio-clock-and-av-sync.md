# 0023 — Real-time playback: audio master clock, sequential decoding, chunked audio scheduling

- Status: Accepted
- Date: 2026-11-07

## Context
Prompt 13 adds real-time playback to the engine of ADR-0022: play a multi-track 1080p sequence with sound at its frame rate without audio/video drift, JKL shuttle, loop over in/out, frame steps, VU meters and a performance HUD. Open questions: which clock drives playback and how pictures follow it; how frames are decoded fast enough (prompt 12's `getSample` configures a decoder per frame); how sound is decoded and scheduled sample-accurately for long durations and across loop points; what plays at speeds other than 1×; how the meter runs off the main thread under the renderer's CSP; and how the timeline and the viewer follow playback without React re-renders.

## Decision
- **The audio clock is the master clock.** `audioContextClock(context)` returns the context time of the sample being *heard* (`getOutputTimestamp()` interpolated with `performance.now()`, never ahead of `currentTime`, never going back). A `Transport` is an anchor (sequence time at a clock time) and a rate; every position is derived from the anchor and the clock (`transportPosition`), never accumulated, so playback cannot drift. New anchors are set `leadTime` ahead (output latency + 30 ms) so the first sample is scheduled in time; the picture holds the start frame meanwhile.
- **`PlaybackController`** (pure, `@se/engine/playback`): play/pause, stop (back to where playback started), JKL (`nextShuttleRate`: 1, 2, 4 in each direction; K pauses; K held + J/L steps), loop over the in/out span (the whole content without one; starting outside the span enters it), stop at the content edges otherwise. On each display refresh it reads the clock and reports the frame to show when it changes: late frames are **skipped**, never waited for. It picks the frame for the **next refresh** (`RefreshPeriod`, the measured refresh interval): what is drawn now reaches the screen then. A playhead moved elsewhere is a seek: playback continues from it.
- **Sequential decoding for playback.** The decoder pool keeps one `FrameReader` per clip on screen or coming up (Mediabunny `samples()`, one decoder running through the file), fed by `prefetch(requests)` on every played frame: kept and extended while the playhead moves forward, restarted after a jump or when more than 0.5 s behind (`planRead`). Reverse playback reads the window before the playhead in chunks (decoders only run forward). Readers are bounded by a **read-ahead budget in frames and bytes** (75 % of the cache shared between readers: a 1080p frame is 8 MB), and **forward playback closes the frames it has passed**: hardware decoders stall when the frames they produced stay open. The window ahead adapts (`PrefetchWindow`: 0.5 s, ×1.5 on a late frame up to 2 s, back down after 3 s on time). During playback a frame waiting for decoding is never superseded (that could starve the screen); the latest time is drawn when it is done. Frames drawn and skipped feed the HUD (`PlaybackStatsCounter`).
- **Sound in one-second chunks.** `AudioChunkPool` decodes chunk `k` (media seconds `[k, k+1)`) with Mediabunny's `AudioBufferSink` and copies it into one `AudioBuffer`. Containers may round audio timestamps to the millisecond (Matroska, WebM) and Mediabunny stamps the same buffer differently depending on where decoding started: buffers are placed **contiguously** unless a real gap shows, and each chunk continues the placement of the previous chunk of the file (decoded in order, the straddling buffer recognised within 1 ms), so chunks join on the sample. Media time 0 of a sound is the first video frame when the file has video (the picture's origin), else its first sample. Audio plays the original or the mezzanine (`playbackSource`), never the proxy.
- **Scheduling 200 ms ahead.** `AudioScheduler` ticks every 25 ms (`setInterval`, not the display refresh) and starts the pieces of sound whose start enters the next 200 ms (`planPieces`): a piece is a chunk of a clip cut at clip edges and where playback stops or wraps (`playbackSegments` splits the clock window at loop points). A piece plays to its end even past the window, so sound is never cut mid-chunk; start times derive from the segment's anchor. Chunks are decoded 2 s ahead; a chunk decoded late joins in time (trimmed). Any jump stops every voice and plans again.
- **Graph.** Voice → clip gain (static volume; keyframes sampled once per piece until prompt 14) → track gain (mute, solo, volume) → track `StereoPannerNode` → master → speakers; track settings use a 10 ms smoothing so mute/solo/volume act immediately.
- **Speeds.** Sound plays only at real-time forward speed (rate 1); 2×, 4× and reverse are silent. Clip speed changes pitch (`playbackRate`).
- **Audio scrub.** A paused playhead move plays an 80 ms snippet (5 ms fades) at the new time, at most every 50 ms (the latest move waits); frame steps scrub too; a preference turns it off.
- **Level meter in an AudioWorklet.** `meter-processor.ts` accumulates peak and RMS per channel on the audio thread and posts about 30 reports per second (`LevelAccumulator`, allocation-free per block). The module is bundled as its own file with Vite's `?worker&url` (an ES module in dev, an IIFE asset in builds) and loaded by `audioWorklet.addModule` from `'self'`: no change to the CSP. The viewer shows RMS bars and held sample peaks (`updatePeakHold`) refreshed 20 times per second, re-rendering only the meter.
- **App wiring.** `PlaybackSession` (viewer feature, outside React) owns the controller and the lazily imported audio engine (`@se/engine/audio`; without Web Audio playback runs silent on the performance clock), writes the playhead store, treats other playhead moves as seeks, and switches the picture engine to playback decoding. A shared `playbackStatusStore` lets the timeline page-scroll while playing. Readouts that change every frame (timecodes) live in leaf components; nothing else re-renders per frame.
- **A/V check.** A browser test generates a « flash + beep » media in Chromium with Mediabunny (no fixture), renders the scheduled sound offline (every beep on its sample, clip at 0 s and 600 s) and plays it in real time (each flash drawn within 45 ms of its beep; measured −10 to +7 ms on macOS).

## Consequences
- Picture and sound share one clock: no drift whatever the duration; a 10-minute schedule is exact to the sample (unit test).
- Two 1080p H.264 tracks play at 29.97 fps without dropped frames on Apple silicon (was 9 fps before byte budgets and drop-behind).
- Playback depends on timers in the renderer: a hidden or minimised window throttles them (backlog).
- Reverse playback of long-GOP footage decodes a window per chunk: smooth for short GOPs, choppier otherwise (skipped frames are counted).
- Volume automation and fades (prompt 14) will turn clip gains into ramps scheduled with the pieces.

## Alternatives considered
- **`performance.now()` as master clock, audio slaved to it**: audio devices run on their own crystal; correcting audio continuously causes clicks or drift. The audio clock is what the ear hears.
- **`AudioContext.currentTime` without output timestamps**: it leads what is heard by the output latency (tens of ms on Bluetooth), so pictures would run early.
- **`getSample` per frame for playback**: one decoder configuration and a decode from the key frame per frame; measured far below real time at 1080p.
- **One `AudioBufferSourceNode` per decoded buffer**: ~47 voices per second per clip and cuts at every scheduling window; one-second chunks cut once per second, exactly.
- **Stringified worklet code through a `blob:` URL**: needs `blob:` in `script-src`; `?worker&url` keeps the CSP unchanged.
- **Silence-free fast shuttle (pitch-shifted or granular audio at 2×/4×)**: more work, little value before editing needs it (backlog).
